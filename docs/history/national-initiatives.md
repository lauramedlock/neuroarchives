# National and Multinational Brain Initiatives

This section covers the government-level programmes and the intergovernmental bodies that coordinate them, from the
first neuroinformatics programme in 1993 to the national initiatives launched from 2013 onward. Of the eight major
government-level efforts of the 2013–2018 wave, four are actively funded (US, EU via EBRAINS, Japan, China), two are re-scoping toward AI and neurotechnology (Korea, China), and two have never secured a dedicated national budget (Canada, Australia).

## US Human Brain Project, NIMH (1993–2004)

- **Proposed:** The first national neuroinformatics programme, funded from 1993 by NIMH with partner agencies. Its
  premise was that neuroscience's bottleneck was not data collection but data description: results were not recorded in
  any form a second laboratory could reuse. It funded databases, tools and vocabularies rather than instruments.
- **Created:** The first generation of neuroscience databases and the tooling around them, several of which outlived
  the programme — [ModelDB](../resources/models.md) (Yale SenseLab, 1996) is still the main archive of published
  computational models. It also established the case for international coordination that produced INCF.
- **Status:** Completed in 2004.
- **Outlook:** Its legacy is the archive-and-standards layer rather than any single resource: the argument that shared
  formats are a precondition for reuse, which BIDS and NWB won two decades later.

## US BRAIN Initiative (2013–present)

- **Proposed:** Announced in 2013 to build tools to record and manipulate brain circuits at scale. The [BRAIN 2025 report](https://www.the-scientist.com/brain-initiative-asks-for-45b-37364) asked for $4.5 billion over roughly ten years; [BRAIN 2.0](https://www.nih.gov/brain/research/tools-technologies-brain-cells-circuits/brain-initiative-cell-atlas-network) (2019) added three "transformative projects": a human cell atlas, whole-brain connectivity, and genetic tools to target cell types.
- **Created:** The BICCN mouse cell census, its successor [BICAN](https://brain-map.org/consortia/bican) (11 grants, \~$100 million a year for 5 years), BRAIN CONNECTS, the Armamentarium, data archives ([DANDI, NeMO, Brain Image Library](../resources/federated-archives.md)), and many brain-computer interface and deep-brain-stimulation trials. NIH leads, with NSF, DARPA and FDA as partners.
- **Status:** Operating but shrinking. Funding fell to [$402 million in FY2024](https://iocdf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/FY26-BRAIN-Sign-On-Letter_.pdf) (about 40% below FY2023) and [$321 million in FY2025](https://braininitiative.nih.gov/funding/understanding-brain-initiative-budget). FY2026 recovered to [$429 million](https://www.thetransmitter.org/funding/neuroscience-brain-initiative-gain-budget-in-bad-nih-funding-bill/), but 21st Century Cures Act money ends after FY2026.
- **Outlook:** Advocates warn a [flat FY2027 budget means a $195 million cut](https://www.caregiveraction.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FY27-BRAIN-Sign-On-Letter-Final-1.pdf). The program continues as long as Congress funds the 10 participating NIH institutes, but at a smaller scale and with new NIH limits on foreign collaboration.

## EU Human Brain Project → EBRAINS (2013–present)

- **Proposed:** A 10-year, \~€1 billion EU flagship (2013–2023) to simulate the human brain on supercomputers. A 2014 open letter from European neuroscientists forced a governance overhaul and a pivot toward building shared infrastructure.
- **Created:** [EBRAINS](https://ebrains.eu/), launched in 2019 as the HBP's legacy: multilevel human brain atlases (Julich-Brain), The Virtual Brain simulator, neuromorphic computing systems, a data and knowledge graph, and federated supercomputing access — all catalogued in the [resource list](../resources/federated-archives.md).
- **Status:** Active. The HBP ended in 2023; [EBRAINS 2.0](https://ebrains.eu/impact/projects/ebrains-20) received €38 million through 2026. A new €32 million Horizon Europe call for EBRAINS services closed in June 2026.
- **Outlook:** EBRAINS is moving from project grants to a permanent European Digital Infrastructure Consortium; [EBRAINS-Implement](https://ebrains.eu/impact/projects/ebrains-implement) (June 2026–Nov 2027) is setting up its legal form. A [10-year roadmap (2026–2036)](https://ebrains.eu/sites/default/files/2026-01/EBRAINS-Roadmap-Call-Details.pdf) is due for adoption in Q1 2027.

## Canadian Brain Research Strategy, CBRS (2015–present)

- **Proposed:** A grassroots coalition (from 2015) seeking a national brain strategy with four pillars — understand, address, apply, build — and a strong neuroethics and Indigenous-knowledge component. Its ask is a [Canadian Brain Research Initiative: $150 million over three years](https://nationalnewswatch.com/2025/03/10/make-brain-health-a-priority-canadians-are-ready-to-vote-for-it).
- **Created:** A national network of 40+ institutes, funders and charities, funded in part by a [$1.5 million CIHR grant](https://centreforbrainhealth.ca/news/15-million-develop-canadian-brain-research-strategy) (2020). It also hosted the IBI secretariat at UBC. Canada's working infrastructure sits alongside it: Brain Canada, the Canada First Research Excellence Fund brain programs, and [Canadian Open Neuroscience Platform \[CONP\]](../resources/federated-archives.md) (full entry in the resource list).
- **Status:** Active as an advocacy and coordination body; no dedicated federal initiative funded as of October 2026. Neuroscience groups continued [pressing for it in 2026 pre-budget submissions](https://can-acn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/CAN-pre-budget-submission-May-2026.pdf).
- **Outlook:** Depends on a federal budget commitment; without one, Canada participates through CIHR grants, Brain Canada grants, Canada First Research Excellence Fund \[CFREF\] programs and international partners.

## China Brain Project (2016–present)

- **Proposed:** "Brain Science and Brain-Inspired Intelligence," listed in the 13th Five-Year Plan and structured as "one body, two wings": basic circuit research, plus brain disorders and brain-inspired AI. Funding guidelines exceeded [3.1 billion yuan](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/mr-2022-0014/html) when large-scale calls began in 2021.
- **Created:** New institutes (Chinese Institute for Brain Research in Beijing; Shanghai centres), whole-brain imaging facilities, the [Beinao BCI chips](https://bioethics.com/?p=97080), and in July 2025 [ten mesoscale atlas papers](https://cebsit.cas.cn/xwen/mtsm/2019n/202507/t20250718_7892485.html) extending cell and connection maps to macaques.
- **Status:** Active and expanding. In September 2025 China launched the [International Consortium for Primate Brain Mapping](https://www.ion.ac.cn/xwen/mtsm/2019n/202510/t20251020_7992591.html), a 25-year plan with nine member institutions in seven countries and 100+ scientists from 25 countries; US institutions have not joined.
- **Outlook:** The strongest growth trajectory of any national program, with a parallel industrial push on brain-computer interfaces.

## Japan Brain/MINDS → Brain/MINDS 2.0 (2014–present)

- **Proposed:** Brain/MINDS (2014–2024) aimed to map marmoset brain structure and function as a bridge to human disease; Brain/MINDS Beyond added human-marmoset comparisons.
- **Created:** Marmoset brain atlases and transgenic models; the [3D Digital Marmoset Brain Atlas v2.0](https://brainminds.jp/en/news) was released in November 2025.
- **Status:** Active. [Brain/MINDS 2.0](https://brainminds.jp/en) is a six-year program (from 2024) led by [RIKEN](https://cbs.riken.jp/en/) (Tokyo) with seven partner institutions, with 97 subprojects selected in 2024 and 30 more in 2025.
- **Outlook:** Its central goal is a "Digital Brain" — integrating multi-species data into computational brain models — making it the national program most explicitly aimed at a unified brain model.

## Korea Brain Initiative (2016–present)

- **Proposed:** A national plan under Korea's Brain Research Promotion Act, focused on mapping brain functions (notably emotion and decision-making) and developing neuro-tools.
- **Created:** The Korea Brain Research Institute (KBRI, founded 2011) as the national hub, and the [Korea Brain Bank Network](https://k-brainnet.kbri.re.kr/eng/home/index.do).
- **Status:** Active and re-scoping. In March 2026 the science ministry [announced an AI-led brain industry program](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2454334) covering BCIs, neurotherapeutics and brain-data infrastructure.
- **Outlook:** Shifting from mapping toward industry and brain data for AI.

## Australian Brain Alliance (2016–present)

- **Proposed:** An Australian Brain Initiative, costed at [$500 million over five years](https://science.org.au/node/3698), to "crack the brain's code" and build a neurotechnology industry.
- **Created:** A coalition of [28+ member organisations](https://ans.org.au/resources/issues/about-the-australian-brain-alliance?Itemid=111) under the Australian Academy of Science, plus a published neuroethics framework.
- **Status:** Active as an advocacy body; no dedicated national initiative has been funded. Its co-chair, Anthony Hannan, now chairs the IBI.

## International Neuroinformatics Coordinating Facility, INCF (2005–present)

- **Proposed:** Set up after the OECD pushed for international coordination of neuroscience data sharing, as the
  standing body the field had lacked — somewhere formats and best practices could be reviewed and endorsed rather than
  each group inventing its own.
- **Created:** [Founded in Stockholm in 2005 by seven OECD countries](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/58454);
  it now endorses community standards — [BIDS and NWB](../resources/standards-catalogs.md) among them — and runs open
  training materials.
- **Status:** Active; [co-hosted by KTH Stockholm](https://neurotecheu.umh.es/en/?p=1741) and member-funded.
- **Outlook:** Being member-funded makes it small but durable: it depends on national nodes paying subscriptions rather
  than on any single grant cycle, which is why it has outlasted the programme that created it.

## International Brain Initiative (2017–present)

- **Proposed:** Announced on [8 December 2017](https://internationalbraininitiative.org/about) to coordinate the national programs on data sharing, standards, ethics and training.
- **Created:** Working groups on data sharing, neuroethics, brain mapping and cross-cultural issues; affiliates now include Latin American, African and New Zealand initiatives.
- **Status:** Active; independent of the Kavli Foundation since 2022, with a secretariat at UBC through 2026. It held a [2026 summit](https://www.internationalbraininitiative.org/news/o2e9cifjzmhdvr1f5yhmbizlh8s3kh).
- **Outlook:** Its secretariat term ends in 2026, so its next host and funding are an open question.

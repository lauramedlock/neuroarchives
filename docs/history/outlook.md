# Outlook

The field is shifting from time-limited megaprojects to permanent infrastructure plus AI models, while leadership spreads from the US and EU toward China and Japan.

- **Funding is the main US risk.** BRAIN's special Cures Act money ends after FY2026. Unless Congress raises base funding, FY2027 brings a cut of about $195 million, which would slow BICAN, CONNECTS and the archives that store their data.
- **Europe is institutionalizing.** EBRAINS is becoming a permanent legal consortium with a 10-year roadmap — the most stable long-term home for shared brain data and models.
- **China is scaling fastest.** The China Brain Project and the 25-year ICPBM are building primate and human atlases with their own data platform, which may split the ecosystem into US-aligned and China-aligned poles.
- **Canada and Australia remain unfunded at the national level.** Both have mature coalitions and strategies ready; each needs a budget decision. Canada's CBRS asks for $150 million over three years.
- **Simulation is going non-profit and open.** With Blue Brain closed, detailed brain simulation now depends on the Open Brain Institute, EBRAINS and Japan's Digital Brain.
- **Foundation models are the new integrator.** TRIBE v2, the MICrONS model and the Enigma Project show that one model can generalize across people, animals and tasks. The next test is joining scales: cell atlases, connectomes, recordings and whole-brain imaging in a single system.

Open question to track: whether the IBI secures a new secretariat and funding after its UBC term ends in 2026.
